Suggested bindings and paybacks

Two halves of one loop. At your mailbox, Mailworthy suggests sending materials to the crafters around you: who's in your party (guild members included, their profession from the roster) and your own alts. At the crafter's mailbox, once your materials arrive, their Mailworthy suggests what they could make that you'd use (an 8-slot bag when your bags hold 6), and sending it back counts in the mail ledger, so what they owe you goes down a little with each payback. Nothing is ever mailed until a player picks it, sees it in the preview and presses Send.

Who gets suggested

  • Your party and your alts only (operator, 2026-11-22): your alts (their saved professions, as the own-crafter offer does now) and party / raid members, when Mailworthy knows a crafting profession of theirs that takes something on this list that no binding takes yet. A party member's profession comes from their Mailworthy's hello (H2 / S2 on PARTY) or, for a guild member, from the roster's profession1ID / profession2ID (Brann: no Mailworthy needed). Guild members who aren't in your party aren't suggested.
  • Tags say why you know them, and they combine: Grace is PartyGuildMW. Order: in your party first (party and guild above party alone), then your alts. Three at a time, More pages.
  • A party member with neither (Tobb) is listed as "professions unknown": the game doesn't show another player's professions.
  • Never you, your bank alt, a profession you have yourself (me first: your Mining keeps its ore), or one a binding already names. "Not for Grace" is remembered, as today's "Not for Sadie" is.
  • The count is what the binding would really take, through Routes.Divert (hard keeps, the cap, tiers and the player's real skill all count), as Routes.Offer counts it today. Rough Stone shows the point: it was Staying (not worth vendoring), and Dorn takes it.

Accept for this visit, or bind

  • This visit: a binding that lasts until the mailbox closes (like Skip once, in reverse). For the stranger in your dungeon group. Bind: an ordinary binding (Routes.Add), as today's offer.
  • Either way the rows move to Mailing under that player with their "to Grace" chip, and the To: lines, postage and Send button update before anything goes. Unbind or Undo puts them back.
  • Only your side's balance changes: Grace now owes you, and Mailworthy never shows you that.

Where the ideas come from

  • What Grace can make: her own Tailoring window, read by the tier check she already runs (TierCheck reads every learned recipe's reagents and output today; it would save the outputs and reagent counts too).
  • What Aldric could use: when Aldric's Mailworthy mails counted materials to Grace, it whispers her a short needs note with the item values (M2): his level, class, bag sizes (4 x 6 slots), what his ranged weapon fires, empty gear slots. Only to a player he just mailed, only Mailworthy to Mailworthy, and he can turn it off.
  • Match: an idea is a recipe Grace knows whose output Aldric could use by a fixed rule per kind (view 3): a bag bigger than his smallest; something for an empty slot he can wear; ammo his gun fires; food, potions or bandages for his level. Never "an upgrade" guessed from stats.
  • Not suggested lines say what was left out and why, so a missing idea isn't a mystery.

Crafting stays Grace's

  • Mailworthy never crafts. "Craft one" here stands for Grace making it in her Tailoring window. The idea shows which materials she has (a bolt from his wool counts) and what's missing.
  • Once the item is in her bags, its row has the pick button: Me → Aldric. One click makes it a payback for this visit: a To: line, a PB "back to Aldric" chip, and the meter's striped part shows how much it would pay back. Nothing goes before Send.

The ledger clears slowly

  • A payback is an ordinary counted mail: the ledger already lowers what Grace owes by what she sends Aldric. Each bag takes a bite out of the meter; the debt never has to be paid in one go. Part paid logs something done in person, and Mark paid still settles the rest.
  • A payback is valued like any mail, at its auction price. A crafted item with no price (the shirt) is valued at its materials, and Aldric's side counts that value only up to what he'd sent Grace: a payback can clear a debt, never make one.
  • Only Grace's own side shows. Aldric gets "Grace Thorn made you a Woolen Bag" in chat; he never sees what she owed.

Upgrades to try (proposal)

  • At the profession window: opening Tailoring prints one chat line with the ideas she can make right now (Open your Tailoring window). TierCheck already listens to that window's events; nothing is written into Blizzard's window.
  • Fold the ideas block to one line (▾ / ▸), as the suggestions block does.

Paid in person: Mark part paid

  • An enchant cast on Aldric, a trade or gold in hand doesn't go through the mailbox. Part paid logs an amount (− / + step it, 5s each), and the balance drops by that much. It takes two presses ("Sure? 20s"), like Mark paid, and is logged in the export. Mark paid still clears the rest.

Rules every idea keeps

  • Fixed checks from item data (bag slots, item class and subclass, required level, armor type), never stat weights: an idea is a guess you can read and check ("his smallest bag holds 6"), not a verdict.
  • Level band: the item's required level is at or under the sender's level and no more than 10 under it.
  • The sender's needs note is a snapshot from their last counted mail, shown with its age ("as of 2 days ago"); a newer one replaces it, and it's dropped after 30 days.
  • Each idea can be turned down for that player ("Not for Aldric"), and the whole feature has a switch (/mw feature paybacks off) and a setting for the needs note.
